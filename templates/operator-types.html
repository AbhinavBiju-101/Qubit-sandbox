Three definitions, in matrix arithmetic

M† means "conjugate-transpose M" — flip it and conjugate every entry (exactly the bra-from-ket operation, applied to a whole matrix). Three properties, three roles:

HermitianM† = M — observables. Guarantees real eigenvalues, so measurement outcomes are always real numbers.
UnitaryM†M = I — gates. Guarantees the state stays normalized; every gate you've ever clicked is one.
ProjectionM² = M — measurement. Applying it twice does nothing new, exactly like "already collapsed, stays collapsed."
A matrix can be more than one at once — Z is both Hermitian and unitary, which is exactly why it can be measured (as an observable) or applied (as a gate).

Bonus: the odd one out

[[1,1],[0,1]] is neither Hermitian, unitary, nor a projection — which is exactly why it never shows up as a gate or an observable anywhere in this app. Every legitimate quantum gate has to be unitary; a matrix that isn't simply doesn't correspond to any physically realizable operation on a qubit.
