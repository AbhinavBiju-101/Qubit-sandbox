Losing information to the environment

Every gate in this app has been a clean, reversible rotation — the state vector's length never changes. Real qubits aren't perfectly isolated: stray photons, thermal vibrations, and imperfect control electronics all slowly leak information out of the qubit and into its surroundings. Once that information is out there, unrecoverable, the qubit's own state can no longer be described as a single clean point on the sphere — it becomes a statistical mix, and the Bloch vector representing it physically shrinks toward the center.

Two numbers describe how fast, and you'll see both named on real hardware specs (Reality Check has actual published values): T1, how long amplitude information survives (energy relaxation), and T2, how long phase information survives (dephasing) — T2 is always ≤ 2×T1, and in practice usually shorter.

A vector at the center of the sphere (length 0) represents total uncertainty — 50/50 no matter which basis you measure in. That's not a superposition anymore; it's just noise. Decoherence is the process of a qubit sliding from "definite quantum state" to "noise," continuously.
