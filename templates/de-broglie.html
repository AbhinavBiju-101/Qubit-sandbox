MODULE 4 · WHERE QUANTUM COMES FROM

Everything has a wavelength. Almost none of them matter.

The same formula gives an electron a wavelength big enough to diffract around atoms, and gives a thrown baseball a wavelength smaller than anything that has ever been measured. Five steps, including where this formula leads next.

1

λ = h / p

de Broglie's 1924 proposal: if light (a wave) can act like a particle, maybe particles can act like waves. The wavelength is λ = h / p, where p = mv is ordinary momentum and h is the same Planck's constant from Blackbody Radiation.

Because h is astronomically small (6.6 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s), only very light, very slow-moving things get a wavelength large enough to notice. That's the whole reason quantum effects feel exotic instead of everyday — the formula applies to everything, but the number only matters for the very small.

This is the wave side of the double slit's particles — an electron passing through those two slits is diffracting exactly because it has a de Broglie wavelength comparable to the slit spacing.
2

A wave trapped in a box has to fit exactly

Here's why de Broglie's formula turns out to be more than a curiosity about wavelengths — it's the seed of energy quantization itself, previewed here and derived properly in Particle in a Box, next. Confine any wave to a fixed region — a guitar string pinned at both ends, or a particle trapped between two walls — and it can only settle into a standing wave that fits: a whole number of half-wavelengths has to span the box exactly, or the wave destructively interferes with itself and cancels out. That's not a quantum rule on its own; it's just what a wave confined between two fixed boundaries has always done, guitar strings included.

Combine that fitting condition, n·(λ/2) = L for a box of length L and whole number n, with de Broglie's λ = h/p, and momentum itself is forced to only take specific values: p = nh/(2L). Since kinetic energy is p²/(2m), energy becomes quantized too — not because someone declared it so, but because a wave that doesn't fit the box isn't a valid standing wave at all. This is the entire mechanism Particle in a Box works out in full, with an actual live spectrum of energy levels.

4

Bonus: back to the double slit

An electron accelerated to a modest speed has a de Broglie wavelength on the order of picometers — close enough to atomic spacing that crystals diffract electrons the same way they diffract X-rays. That's not a coincidence; it's the experimental confirmation that made de Broglie's proposal a real theory instead of a guess.

5

A microscope's resolution is limited by its wavelength

An optical microscope can never resolve detail much finer than the wavelength of the light illuminating it — roughly 400-700 nanometers for visible light, which is why viruses (tens of nanometers) are invisible to one no matter how good the lenses are. An electron microscope sidesteps this by illuminating with electrons instead of light, and an electron accelerated through a typical microscope's voltage has a de Broglie wavelength on the order of a few picometers — roughly 100,000 times shorter than visible light.

That wavelength gap is the entire reason electron microscopes can resolve individual atoms while optical microscopes fundamentally cannot — not better engineering, a genuinely different, much smaller wavelength doing the illuminating.
