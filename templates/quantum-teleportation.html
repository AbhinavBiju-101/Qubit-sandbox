The four-move protocol

Alice has a qubit in some state she doesn't even need to know — call it the "secret." She and Bob also share an entangled Bell pair from earlier (Two Qubits, Step 3). The protocol: (1) Alice entangles her secret with her half of the pair, (2) she measures both of her qubits, getting two classical bits, (3) she sends those two bits to Bob over an ordinary channel, (4) Bob applies a small correction — X, Z, both, or neither, depending on the bits — and his qubit becomes the secret.

Nothing traveled faster than light: Bob's qubit isn't usable until Alice's two classical bits physically arrive. What "teleports" is the state, not information on its own — and Alice's original qubit is left randomized in the process, so this isn't cloning either.

The two classical bits Bob needs are exactly the two measurement outcomes from Alice's Bell-basis measurement — same four possible outcomes as the four Bell states from Two Qubits, just used here to steer a correction instead of being the answer themselves.

Bonus: why Alice can't peek first

If Alice measured her secret directly to "read" it before teleporting, she'd collapse it and could only send classical bits describing one measured outcome — not the full complex state. Teleportation works precisely because Alice never learns α and β; she measures a joint property of her two qubits instead, which is enough to steer Bob's correction without ever revealing the secret itself.
