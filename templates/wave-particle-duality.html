Young's double slit, and the puzzle it left behind

Shine light (or fire electrons) through two narrow, closely-spaced slits at a screen. Waves passing through both slits interfere: crest-meets-crest gives a bright band, crest-meets-trough cancels to dark — a striped fringe pattern, not two simple bands.

The puzzle: this still happens when particles go through one at a time, slow enough that only one particle is ever in flight. Each one lands as a single point — clearly a particle. But over thousands of them, the points build the exact same striped pattern — clearly a wave. Both descriptions are correct; neither is complete alone. That's wave-particle duality.

Every amplitude you've used since Single Qubit already carries this: a state is a wave-like sum of possibilities right up until measurement, at which point it commits to one particle-like outcome. The double slit is that same story, just running in physical space instead of a Bloch sphere.
