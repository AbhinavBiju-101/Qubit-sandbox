From one qubit to two

One qubit has 2 basis states, |0⟩ and |1⟩. Two qubits don't have 2+2 — they have 2×2 = 4: |00⟩, |01⟩, |10⟩, |11⟩. Each one gets its own complex amplitude, so a general 2-qubit state needs 4 numbers, not 2.

That multiplication, not addition, is why qubit count matters so much: 10 qubits describe a state space of 2¹⁰ = 1,024 amplitudes; 20 qubits already need over a million.

If neither qubit is touched by a gate that mixes them, the two behave completely independently — that's the "prepare and measure" case in Step 2. Step 3's bonus is what happens once a gate does mix them.

Bonus: what entanglement changes

Minimum gates for a fixed basis state is one story. Here's the more interesting one: apply H to qubit 1, then CNOT from qubit 1 onto qubit 2. Neither qubit has a definite value on its own anymore — but measuring them is no longer independent, either.
