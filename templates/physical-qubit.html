2

How it's controlled

Microwave pulses tuned to the transmon's transition frequency (typically 4–6 GHz) are sent down a control line. The pulse's length and phase set the rotation angle and axis on the Bloch sphere — the same H / X / RY logic from the Single Qubit lesson, just implemented as a physical pulse instead of a button click.

3

How it's measured

A probe tone is sent into the coupled resonator. Its resonant frequency shifts slightly depending on whether the qubit is in |0⟩ or |1⟩ (dispersive readout). The reflected signal is amplified by a cryogenic amplifier, then digitized at room temperature to yield a 0 or 1.

4

Bonus: why so cold

Dilution refrigerators cool the chip to ~10–15 mK — colder than deep space. Below, a qubit is prepared in an equal superposition and left alone. Watch what raising the temperature does to how long it stays coherent.

What happens if temperature rises significantly: thermal energy starts randomly kicking the qubit between |0⟩ and |1⟩ (bit flips), stray thermal photons fill the resonator and add noise to every readout, and if the chip rises past the material's superconducting critical temperature the Josephson junction stops being a junction at all — it's just a resistor, and there is no qubit left to measure.
