What "|0⟩" actually is, physically.

A superconducting qubit chain: Josephson junction → transmon → resonator → readout. Click a stage for what it does and why it's there.

HARDWARE CHAIN
DETAIL

Josephson Junction

HOW IT'S CONTROLLED

Microwave pulses tuned to the transmon's transition frequency (typically 4–6 GHz) are sent down a control line. The pulse's length and phase set the rotation angle and axis on the Bloch sphere — this is literally the same H / X / RY logic from the Single Qubit Lab, just implemented as a physical pulse instead of a button click.

HOW IT'S MEASURED

A probe tone is sent into the coupled resonator. Its resonant frequency shifts slightly depending on whether the qubit is in |0⟩ or |1⟩ (dispersive readout). The reflected signal is amplified by a cryogenic amplifier, then digitized at room temperature to yield a 0 or 1.

WHY SO COLD · LIVE DECOHERENCE DEMO

Turn up the temperature

Dilution refrigerators cool the chip to ~10–15 mK — colder than deep space. Below, a qubit is prepared in an equal superposition and left alone. Watch what raising the temperature does to how long it stays coherent.

Bonus — what happens if temperature rises significantly: thermal energy starts randomly kicking the qubit between |0⟩ and |1⟩ (bit flips), stray thermal photons fill the resonator and add noise to every readout, and if the chip rises past the material's superconducting critical temperature the Josephson junction stops being a junction at all — it's just a resistor, and there is no qubit left to measure.
